01 What does DTCP approval mean for me as a buyer? *
Directorate of Town & Country Planning approval confirms the layout's plan — roads, plot boundaries and common areas — meets state norms before a single plot is sold. We publish the approval number for each project on its page. (Answer to be confirmed by client.)
02 Can nationalised banks fund these plots? *
DTCP-approved layouts with clear titles are generally eligible for plot loans from major banks. We'll share the exact lenders currently working with each project. (Answer to be confirmed by client.)
03 Will I get patta in my name?
Yes — supporting the transfer of patta to your name after registration is part of the standard process we handle for every buyer.
04 Can I visit the site before booking?
Always, and we insist on it. Site visits run Monday to Saturday, 8.00–18.00. Call 044 4208 7913 to book a guided walk of the actual plots.

Design rationale — Screen 01 · Homepage

  1. What is borrowed. Anehana: the near-invisible glass header dissolving over a full-bleed land visual, the animated counters, and the geographic band whose markers lead onward to projects. Hosachiguru: editorial whitespace and the asymmetric project showcase (one hero project + two supporting) instead of a uniform card row. G Square: only the content idea of explicit value propositions — rewritten in VSS's own voice.
  2. How it is adapted. The hero is the client-approved concept render (aerial-hero.jpg) with the validated Meridian overlay gradient, headline set bottom-left with a quiet scroll-cue/geo rail on the right — the "show the ground" rule stays intact. Counters animate with tabular numerals inside the approved gold-rule stat band, which now overlaps the hero edge. Per review: plot availability is no longer a homepage section — it lives as value block 02 ("Choose your plot before you visit", linking to the dedicated plot-map screen 04). Markers on the geographic band sit on a stylised map underlay (corridor + coastline hint) and remain illustrative until client-confirmed coordinates arrive.
  3. Why it improves on the previous proposal. The approved Direction-A mockup read as a conventional template: centred hero, flat stat row, three equal cards. This composition adds rhythm (full-bleed → overlapping band → asymmetric showcase → numbered values → geographic full-bleed → timeline → FAQ → CTA), alternates dense and sparse sections, and sells plot availability — VSS's actual product — as a one-line value step rather than a heavy section.
  4. Client input still required. All starred values: years in business, families served, acres developed, per-project locations, extents, plot counts/sizes/prices, DTCP approval numbers (the signboard photos show "26/2016" and "50/2021" — confirm before publishing), availability counts, FAQ answers, and marker coordinates. Aerial hero is a concept render — client must supply real drone photography per project before launch.
